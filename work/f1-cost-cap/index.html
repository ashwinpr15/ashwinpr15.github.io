Did Formula 1's cost cap level the grid?

A team analysis of constructor points, wins and podiums across 11 pre-cap seasons (2010–2020) and 5 post-cap seasons (2021–2025).

Course
BA780 Introduction to Data Analytics
Team
B05, four members
When
Fall 2026
Tools
Python, pandas, SciPy, Matplotlib

The top two teams' average points share fell 2.58 percentage points (54.72% → 52.14%), and the comparison was inconclusive (Welch t = −1.215, p = 0.245).

Bottom line: a redistribution of success, with limited evidence of broad, lasting parity.

Business problem

Formula 1 introduced a budget cap in 2021, in part to narrow the gap between the richest teams and the rest. We asked whether it helped more teams compete near the front, and what that means for the FIA, the teams, sponsors and broadcasters.

My role

Team B05: Adrian Marcell, Ashwin Pillanda Ravindra, Jenniffer Hierro Cruz and Saleha Hassan Alvi.

Your specific contribution to the project (for example the data pipeline, the statistical tests, the charts, the recommendations or the presentation).

Approach

  1. Download a fixed F1DB data release (v2026.15.1) and check its SHA-256 before use.
  2. Audit missing values and duplicates, join constructors, standings and race results, and document team renames.
  3. Compute each constructor's share of season points and check that each season totals 100%.
  4. Compare 2010–2020 with 2021–2025, giving every season equal weight, with extra checks on the baseline period and on individual seasons.
  5. Explain the results with descriptive statistics, exploratory Welch tests and 15 charts.

Findings

  • 54.72% → 52.14%Top-two points share, before vs after the cap
  • p = 0.245Welch test on that change: inconclusive
  • 69.79%Big Three share after the cap if 2025 is excluded, above the pre-cap 67.51%
  • 5.18 → 6.00Teams reaching a podium per season
  • 3.18 → 3.20Teams winning a race per season
  • +7.48 ppMcLaren's gain in average points share, the largest

Recommendations

  • FIA and F1: review several balance measures, both baselines, and results with and without the latest season before changing financial rules.
  • Constructors: set targets against relevant improving peers.
  • Sponsors: shortlist emerging contenders and agree performance conditions.
  • Broadcasters: plan for both close and dominant seasons. Post-cap title margins ranged from 0.53 pp (2024) to 18.35 pp (2023).

Limitations

Five post-cap seasons is a small sample, and the 2022 technical reset (new ground-effect cars plus aerodynamic testing limits) overlaps the cap, so its effect can't be isolated. Points share measures sporting results, not spending or car speed, and the supporting tests are exploratory and not adjusted for multiple comparisons.
